# Changelog

## V1.2.0
- App Store: Ziggurat's app store on the Agent (starts off; switch it on in Control). While it's on, each character's phone shows only the apps installed on it: everyone starts with the free Ziggurat apps (Agent, The Garden, Ask Alex, Notes, Calendar, the App Store) and gets the rest from the store, even apps they had before. Featured shows each app's publisher, price ("Free", "§10", "§50/mo") and a rules line, with Get, Buy or Subscribe: buying pays through BangMe! and the app drops onto the home screen; a bought app reinstalls free. Installed lists what's on the Agent (Open, Uninstall); Subscriptions lists monthly apps (Cancel, Renew). A lapsed subscription keeps the app, locked behind Renew. Needs a signal. The GM's App Store page sets each app's price model (free, one-time, monthly), price, where it's offered (the Pool, Fixer only, GM only), whether it's preinstalled, its publisher and rules line; adds "coming soon" listings for apps that don't exist; and per character installs an app free, removes one with a reason ("Bricked by a virus") and an optional refund, manages subscriptions (lapse, reactivate, sponsor, cancel), sends the monthly subscriptions bill (nothing bills by itself; a badge counts what's due), and sideloads: a Fixer's app package in a conversation, installed when its money request is paid (or with Install, when free). Switch the App Store off to put every app back on every phone. Macro API: `pool.*` and `subscriptions.*` (another module, such as CPR HQ, can bill subscriptions instead), hook `subscriptionsChanged`.
- REO Meatwagon: every world now starts with a "REO Meatwagon" service, offered to everyone, with a Request Pickup button and a REO Meatwagon call line that rings the GM (answering as the ambulance service). Added once by the active GM; edit or delete it in Agent Services like any other and it stays that way.
- Agent Monitor (GM): a see-through window (opacity slider, solid under the pointer) with every player character's Agent: out or put away, the app and screen its player is on (who they're texting, which job, which note, the ride they're booking…), when they're typing, and their last actions (texts, calls, payments, searches, pulls, Flappy Chrome runs…), with signal, silent, calls, Cabb rides, WatchBox and Trauma Team alongside. Each app says where they are in it (a Trauma Team plan or emergency, a Flappy Chrome run and its score, an Alex search waiting on a roll…). Dismiss history a line at a time, a character at a time, or hide it all. Click a portrait for the sheet, cut or restore a phone's signal (as in Control), or pan to their token. Opened from the binoculars in the GM Phone's header or a key (unbound at first).
- Jobs: pop a job out of the phone into its own see-through window (as clear as the HUD opacity setting, solid under the pointer) that stays up with the phone away and follows the job live. The GM's has every control the GM Phone has, and can pop it out on every player's screen. It leaves out the status (the GM sets it with the pencil). Players see what their phone shows; it closes if the job is hidden or deleted.
- Gacha: junk capsules, 161 collectibles to find. Pulls cost 160 zCreds, bought with eddies (BangMe!) in bundles at a predatory rate (22-27eb a pull, and never a whole number of pulls); about one purchase in five fails and keeps the eddies. Odds are 68 / 24 / 6 / 1.4 / 0.6%, with pity: the 10th pull without an Epic is Epic or better, and the 90th without a Legendary is Legendary. Each pull is a few unskippable, unreasonably dramatic seconds. Keep collectibles on a shelf, pin up to five favourites to its top, show them off in chat, give them to another character, or sell duplicates back for a zCred or two. The GM sets the prices, bundles, odds and collectibles (an icon or a picture each), sets anyone's zCreds, and can make a Legendary a real item.
- Achieve!: the 30 achievements of R. Talsorian's Achievements and Loot Boxes, with their badges. Each character earns each one once; the GM picks a character on the GM Phone and awards it to them and any others ticked (with a note), and their players get the badge across the screen with a fanfare (eight to choose from in Control → Every phone, or each phone's own). The GM trades one in for a reroll or a Mystery Box (players can ask), takes one back, makes one secret or switches it off, and adds their own. Optional chat announcements. Mr. Amaze's Mystery Boxes have their own tab: a character gets one when an achievement is traded in for it (or the GM gives one), the Table of Mystery picks the box, they open it (1d6), and the GM confirms the item, matched to the Cyberpunk RED compendiums (all 60 are there), before it lands on their sheet.
- Combat Cabb: armored taxis from the phone. Pick where to on the map (the pins on your NAV scene, or a default Night City list) and see the fare: flag fall plus per km, double into a Combat Zone or the Hot Zone, all set by the GM. The fare is charged through BangMe! at pickup, and the cab waits there until the GM sends it off (once the tokens are in). Alerts follow the cab (on its way, here, moving, arrived) and the GM gets a card at each step (here now, depart, arrived now, cancel); the GM moves the tokens. Cabs pick up at the party pin the GM drags on the NAV scene, or a place the GM picks. Only the pins players can see are on the map and in the GM's list (a pin linked to a journal entry or page needs at least Limited). The map drags and zooms (wheel, pinch or its buttons); the GM can preview it, search the place list (30 at a time, for worlds with hundreds of pins), and tap a pin on the map to bring it to the top of the list. A big map picture with a Deep Zoom pyramid beside it (map.jpg → map.dzi + map_files/, as `vips dzsave` makes) is drawn from tiles at the size on screen, instead of the whole picture.
- Flappy Chrome: a flappy game starring a chromed Night City pigeon (smog stacks, Maelstrom gunfire). The world leaderboard (each character's best) shows in the game, and a score that makes it asks for three initials, arcade style; the GM adds made-up names or fills it with randoms, and hears when someone starts and stops playing. It gets harder as you score: the city speeds up (to a cap of 1.4×), openings shrink and vary more, and Maelstrom fires in patterns (tracers, bursts, an aimed laser shot, sweeps, a wall with one way through, a weaving drone), each with a warning where it will come, while the stacks ahead open wider (red rims), the ones already on screen included, so the stack you're in when the shots arrive is wide too. Each character carries Maelstrom Heat (0-5) between sessions, raised by runs of 10/25/40/60/80: it brings the patterns earlier and more often. Players never see it; the GM's Flappy Chrome page lists it, and the GM cools a character down or heats them up a step, resets everyone's, or resets it along with the board. With the game open, the phone won't shrink smaller than the game needs.
- NCART Runner: an endless runner on the phone, along an elevated NCART line at night past the city's towers (starts off; switch it on in Control). Three lanes: A/D or the arrows change lane, W/Space jumps, S slides (or slams down from a jump), P/Esc pauses, M mutes the music; on a touch screen, swipe. The track drops away over a crest ahead, so trouble rises into view late. Grab data shards past police roadblocks, billboards and live cables, burning AV wrecks, parked and oncoming trains (headlights over the crest, then the horn), drones and, later on, gang gunfire from the trackside. Ramps lead up onto train roofs, and some stretches are run roof to roof; others dive through dark megabuilding tunnels (low ceilings: don't ride a roof in). Each run opens close on the runner and pulls out as they sprint up to speed, which then creeps up the further they go; every run is random. Eight power-ups, the same for everyone: Sandevistan (bullet time, everything muffled), Kerenzikov (auto-dodge), Subdermal Armor (one free crash), Jump Booster, Skate Foot, Neural Link (shard magnet), TeleOptics (see further, even in tunnels) and the rare Data Spike (double shards). The world leaderboard ranks by shards (distance breaks ties), each character's best, with arcade initials; shards are for bragging only. The GM manages the board like Flappy Chrome's, picks which obstacles and power-ups appear, and sees runs in the Agent Monitor. A funk loop plays on the phone, with footsteps, gunfire and train horns; the run's sounds play quietly around the player's token, and a train warning also sounds its lane. Reduce motion drops the rain, shake and flashing.
- REDLINE: street racing on the phone, OutRun / Road Rash style, through four districts (Combat Zone gangers and burning wrecks in the dark, Corporate Center traffic and NCPD checkpoints, the Badlands highway's straights, hills, convoys, semis and sandstorms, Pacifica's floods, Maelstrom roadblocks and ramps). Starts off; switch it on in Control. Pick a ride (Roadbike, Compact, Sport or Superbike: speed against grip and toughness) and a plate tag. Sharp bends need the brakes (chevrons warn of them), and the inside of a bend is the fast line. Quick Race: a random 45–90 s sprint against a small pack, starting mid-pack; run it again to race your best three runs of it as ghosts, and nominate a course you liked for the GM. Set Tracks: the GM's circuits (laps) and long sprints, each with a board (best time and best lap, plate tag, ride and grid slot) and ghosts of everyone's best run; up to three load by themselves, with a live split against the fastest. A track's card shows its shape, and its hazards once you've driven past them. A / D steer, S brakes, Q / E punch or kick from a bike (cars ram), P / Esc pauses (drive on, or end the race there), M mutes the music. Gangers, rivals and NCPD show each swing or sideswipe before it lands: steer away or punch first. Rides wreck and burn on the road for the rest of the race; your own wreck puts you back in the middle of the road, rolling, after three seconds. Street racing draws Heat (a star a lap), and ramming civilians and crashing checkpoints add to it: NCPD cruisers come to sideswipe you. With the Drive skill switch on, a Set Track race starts with a Drive Land Vehicle check (the sheet's own roll, in chat) for your grid slot, shown on the track before the lights. The GM builds tracks (district, circuit or sprint, length, laps, difficulty, hazards; reroll the layout or redraw it in a big layout editor, fly through it, publish, retire), manages boards and ghosts, switches fighting and Heat off, can pause every race, and sees races in the Agent Monitor. No money changes hands.
- Roach Race: the 2077 arcade cabinet on the Agent (starts off; switch it on in Control). One button (Space, W, ↑ or a tap): jump; again in the air, jump again; a third time glitches Roach straight back to the ground, untouchable for a moment, then it recharges for a few seconds. Fences, barriers, walls and pits on the ground; crows and griffons in the air mostly miss a horse on the ground, but a diving crow doesn't, and griffons swoop from 1-4. Apples are +20; a carrot puts Roach on two legs, scoring three times as fast (carrots chain). Stages 1-1 to 1-4 go from day to night, then the course loops faster. One life; every stretch of course is proven clearable. No skill roll: a day pass (20eb, the GM's price) through BangMe! gives unlimited rides until in-world midnight. Steeds are looks bought once per character (Chromed Roach, Kiroshi-eyed Night Mare, Militech Warhorse, Neon Unicorn, Samurai Tour Pony; Gold Roach is a GM gift), never faster than Roach. An all-time Hall of Fame with arcade initials. Beating the House Record (3354) tells the GM, who decides on the prize. The GM switches the cabinet out of order or to free play, sets the price, record and prize, grants, takes back and refunds passes, gives steeds, renames and reprices them, manages the board, and sees runs in the Agent Monitor. Reduce motion drops the shake and flashing.
- BREACH: Breach Protocol on the Agent, with a cyberdeck doing the work (starts off; switch it on in Control). The phone turns on its side for it, as for the Map, in 2077's Breach Protocol look (lime code matrix, buffer and "sequence required to upload" panels, the time remaining on top), whatever the phone's skin. The GM sets up Breach Points on the GM Phone: a name, a DV, a 5×5 to 7×7 grid, up to four daemons (a name, a 2-4 code sequence and what it does at the table, which only the GM sees; presets for Backdoor, Control, Eye-Dee, Paydata, Virus, Datamine), who can see it, where (anywhere, or near a token, map pin or Region, like BangMe! pay points) and single use. The GM sends a breach to a character, or a character asks for one nearby and the GM allows or refuses it. The character rolls Backdoor with the sheet's own roll (their equipped cyberdeck's Interface ability): under the DV they're locked out, no grid; over it, every 2 over adds a buffer slot (4 to 8) and Interface rank sets the time (20 s + 5 a rank). The grid plays like 2077's: the first pick is on the top row, then picks alternate column and row, daemons light up as their codes fill the buffer and upload when complete. The time runs in real time from the first pick. "Just roll it" settles a breach on the roll alone (the daemons in order while they fit the buffer). Every grid is fair: all its daemons fit end to end, and the first always fits the smallest buffer. The host checks every pick and keeps the clock. The GM watches breaches on the GM Phone and in the Agent Monitor, and can pause, add 10 s, spike the trace (10 s less) or cut one off. Results come to the GM as a whispered chat card listing each uploaded daemon's outcome to resolve by hand; nothing applies itself. Practice grids for anyone, deck or not, out of combat, with nothing at stake. Keyboard: arrow keys move along the line, Enter picks; the line is outlined and read out.
- Calendar app: Simple Calendar's months on the phone, with the notes you can see. Players add notes for themselves or their crew and delete their own. With CPR HQ, each day shows the character's settled downtime, and an open downtime round shows its status. New setting: Hide Simple Calendar from players.
- Notes app: each character's notes are pages of a "<name> Agent Notes" journal (Agent Notes folder), written on the phone. With CPR HQ, the crew shares Crew Notes pages in their HQ's journal (any crew member can add, edit or delete them; Journal opens the HQ's Journal tab). The GM reads anyone's.
- Ask Alex Anything: the GM can have Claude (Haiku 4.5, your own Anthropic API key) draft each answer, from the Data Pool and the "Locations" folder (it reads the locations it needs, only tells what's public, and lists what it drew from a secret block). Per-question direction and optional web search (in a popup when you draft), and notes for the GM (sources, what came from secrets, era doubts, a suggested roll). The draft is editable and nothing reaches players until you send it. The answer can come in the voice of the asker's Agent personality. The base instructions can be viewed and edited. The key stays in the GM's browser (optionally for this session only). Its settings are under Optional Anthropic API, with the key's Test button beside it.
- Ask Alex Anything: Library Search rolls use the character sheet's roll (dialog, Luck, the system's roll card) with the Agent's +2. The GM sees each asker's Agent and personality, and players see when Alex speaks in it.
- Ask Alex Anything: Data Pool keywords are back, but nothing answers on its own. A journal entry with a line "Data Pool Keywords: …" (or in the "Data Pool" folder) shows under any question that mentions it or its name, as one of its matches; the GM picks one to put its text in the answer (secret blocks left out), asks for a Library Search first, or does neither.
- Home screen: drag an app to arrange it (or Alt and an arrow key on it). Each phone keeps its own order, the GM's too. Ask Alex Anything shows as "Ask Alex" there.
- Chat can say when someone takes out or puts away their Agent (a physical one, not an Internal Agent), with a speech bubble over their token that doesn't move anyone's view. Off until the GM switches it on in Control, for everyone (the bubble shows to those who can see the token) or whispered to the GM.
- Achieve!: show off an earned achievement in chat.
- Share texts to chat: in a conversation (direct or group), the share button picks an unbroken run of up to 8 texts and posts it publicly as a Neon Agent screen in the sharer's colour palette. Players share what their phone shows (their own names for contacts, a hidden caller stays Unknown); the GM Phone shares as the NPC side. The card is built from the real messages, so it can't be faked. On by default; Control can switch it off.
- Long texts are no longer cut off at 2000 characters (about 330 words): a longer text arrives as several bubbles, split at a paragraph or sentence end, with one notification (up to 10 bubbles, about 3300 words). Same for group chats and services. Ask Alex Anything's answers are one bubble of up to 8000 characters (about 1300 words), and a long answer still writes itself out in a few seconds. Questions to Alex can be up to 2000 characters (were 300), in a box that grows to six lines (Enter asks, Shift+Enter starts a new line).
- BangMe! pay points sell goods: the GM drags Items (from the Items directory, a compendium or a sheet) into a pay point and prices them (the item's market price to start). A player who buys one pays through BangMe! and gets a copy in their gear. A deleted item can't be bought, and a failed hand-over is refunded.
- BangMe!: fixed Pay doing nothing for players (the pay dialog failed on submit). Paying a closed pay point, or an empty "Another amount", now says why.

## V1.1.0
- Implemented a basic API, thanks Anthropic.
- Added the ability to disable "Communications", which disables Contacts, Phone & Texting.
- Added a "Functional Agent Macros" compendium with the API.md recipes as ready-made macros.
- BangMe! pay points can be anchored to a token, map pin or Region: players only see and pay them when nearby.
- API: Trauma Team coverage is readable and changeable by other modules (`trauma.status`, `trauma.plans`, `trauma.update`, and the `traumaChanged` hook).
- Phone sounds: 22 bundled CC0 sounds (text tones, ringtones, money transfer, WatchBox alarms). Each phone picks its own in Settings → Look (heard in the world too); the GM sets every phone's defaults in Control → Every phone, or picks their own file.
- Settings cleanup: much less explanatory text; quick replies and the reaction keyboard are in Messages' settings (the gear), now on players' phones too; clearer names (Navigation Method, Show Lockscreen, Merge Feed & News, Blood overlay when hurt, No sales offers).
- Moved settings onto the phones: in-world text tone/ring and their radius are in Control → Every phone; phone and in-world volume are under Text size in each phone's Look settings.
